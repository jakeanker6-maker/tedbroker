01 General

What is TED Brokers?

TED Brokers is an online trading platform for US-listed stocks, ETFs, options and cryptocurrencies. It includes live charts, watchlists, a screener, order tools, margin accounts, a portfolio view and monthly statements. Copy trading is available as an optional feature.

Which markets can I trade?

US-listed stocks and ETFs (more than 10,000 symbols), options on stocks and ETFs, and 30 cryptocurrencies quoted in USD, including BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE, ADA, LINK and AVAX.

Which languages is the platform available in?

The platform is available in 10 languages: English, Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Chinese, Bengali, Hindi and Russian.

02 Account & verification

What documents do I need to verify my account?

A valid government-issued photo ID (passport, national ID card or driving licence) and a proof of address dated within the last three months, such as a utility bill or bank statement.

What happens after I submit my documents?

We review your documents. If we need a clearer photo or another document, we will email you and state exactly what is missing.

03 Funding & withdrawals

How do I fund my wallet?

Open Wallet and choose Deposit. You can fund by bank transfer or cryptocurrency. For crypto, choose the network and send to the address or scan the QR code shown. The balance is credited after the deposit is approved by our team. PayPal is listed in Wallet but is not available at the moment.

How do I withdraw funds?

Request a withdrawal in Wallet. Funds are held while the request is pending. Enter the 8-character confirmation code we send you, then the request goes for approval by our team. Crypto withdrawals can only go to a saved address that has been verified, and bank accounts must be verified before the first withdrawal.

What is the minimum deposit or withdrawal?

The minimum deposit and withdrawal amount is $10.

What does it cost to trade?

US stocks and ETFs have a $0 commission. Sales carry regulatory pass-through fees (the SEC fee and the FINRA trading activity fee). Options are $0.65 per contract on both buys and sells. Cryptocurrency trades are charged 0.50% of the trade value.

04 Trading tools

What order types can I use?

Market, limit, stop, stop-limit and trailing stop orders. Time-in-force options are day, good-till-cancelled (GTC), immediate-or-cancel (IOC) and fill-or-kill (FOK).

How do options work on the platform?

The Trade page shows the options chain by expiry. You can place single-leg orders or multi-leg strategies from built-in templates, and review the payoff before you confirm. Options quotes can be delayed.

What are ETF baskets?

Ready-made ETF baskets built from five templates, plus custom baskets of up to 20 ETFs. You can buy a basket as one order.

Which tools help me follow the market?

Watchlists, price alerts, recurring investments, the screener, company profiles and the news feed. Find them in the Trade, Screener and News pages.

Can I sell short or trade on margin?

Margin and short-selling are available on eligible accounts. Margin and short sales can increase your losses, and losses on a short sale are not limited. Short sales require shares to be located and can carry a borrow fee. Accounts under $25,000 are subject to pattern day trader limits. Read the full risk disclosure before you use margin or short sales.

What hours can I trade?

US stock and ETF orders follow the US market (NYSE) trading hours and holiday calendar. Cryptocurrency trades on the Trade page as spot only, and cannot be sold short.

Where do I find statements and tax reports?

In Documents: monthly statements, trade confirmations and realized gains reports. These help you keep records and are not tax advice.

05 Investment plans

What investment plans are available?

Robo Advisor, ETF automation, DeFi and Options plans, all in the Plans page. Staking is coming soon. Every plan carries market risk, so read Are plan returns guaranteed? below before you invest.

How do I activate a plan?

Open Plans, choose a plan, and enter an amount from your free cash (withdrawable balance). Each plan has a minimum investment, which is enforced when you activate it.

How do I track my plans?

Plan progress shows each active plan's value, profit or loss, progress and time remaining. Plan history lists past plans.

Are plan returns guaranteed?

No. Plan values follow market prices and are not guaranteed. Past performance is not a reliable guide to future results.

06 Optional copy trading

What is copy trading?

Copy trading is an optional feature. You choose a strategy profile and allocate cash, and your account mirrors the profile's trades in proportion to your allocation. Copy trades run on the same order system as your own orders.

How do I start copying a trader?

Open Copy traders, choose a trader, set the amount you want to allocate and, if you like, a copy stop-loss. The minimum copy amount is $100 by default and the allocation is reserved from your cash while the copy is active.

How do I pause or stop copying?

You can pause and resume a copy at any time. To stop copying, open Copy trading in your dashboard and choose Stop copying. Copied positions are closed at market price and the cash is returned to your account.

What is a copy stop-loss?

A drawdown limit that you choose for a copy. If the copy falls by that amount, copying stops and the copied positions are closed.

Can I lose money with copy trading?

Yes. Copied trades carry market risk, and you could lose some or all of the cash you allocate. Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. Only allocate money you can afford to lose.

07 Security

How do I turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)?

Open Settings and turn on Two-factor authentication. When you sign in, we email a 6-digit code to your registered address and you enter it to finish signing in.

Will I be told about suspicious sign-ins?

Yes. We send an email alert for suspicious sign-ins.

What should I do if I think my account is compromised?

Change your password straight away and make sure 2FA is on. Then contact support by email at support@tedbrokers.com, or by phone at +1 (332) 220-8968 if the account is locked. Signed-in users can also use live chat. We will secure your account and review recent activity with you.

Risk disclosure Trading involves a high risk of loss. Expand to read the full risk disclosure.

This disclosure describes the main risks of trading on the TED Brokers platform and the protections that do and do not apply. It is not investment, tax or legal advice. Read it together with the full risk disclosure, the terms and conditions and the AML / KYC policy.

Market risk

The prices of stocks, ETFs, options and cryptocurrencies can rise or fall quickly and without warning. You can lose some or all of the money you invest. Prices shown on the platform may be delayed, and past performance is not a reliable guide to future results. Interest rate decisions, company news, economic data and events in the United States and abroad can all move prices against you.

Leverage and margin risk

A margin account lets you borrow against the securities in your account. Borrowing increases both gains and losses. Margin requirements follow Regulation T of the Federal Reserve Board and FINRA Rule 4210. If your equity falls below the maintenance requirement, you may receive a margin call with a short deadline. If the call is not met, positions may be sold without your consent, at prices that may be unfavourable to you. You can lose more than the cash you deposited. Withdrawals may be restricted while a margin call is open.

Short selling risk

Short selling means selling shares you have borrowed, in the hope of buying them back at a lower price. Losses are not limited, because a share price can rise without a ceiling. Short sales are subject to the locate requirements of Regulation SHO and to SEC Rule 201, which restricts short sales in a stock that has fallen sharply in a single day. Shares that are hard to borrow can carry a fee that rises over time, and they may become unavailable, which can force you to close the position early.

Options risk

Options can expire worthless, and the premium paid for a long option can be lost in full. Selling options can expose you to losses far larger than the premium received, including unlimited losses on uncovered calls. Multi leg strategies combine several contracts and carry risks that change as prices and time pass. Exercise and assignment can happen before expiry, particularly for American style options on stocks that pay dividends. Before you trade options, read the Options Clearing Corporation publication Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options.

Cryptocurrency risk

Cryptocurrency prices are highly volatile and can fall to zero. Crypto assets are not backed by a government or central bank, and they are not legal tender in every country. Blockchain transactions usually cannot be reversed, and lost access credentials can make assets unrecoverable. Crypto assets are generally not covered by investor compensation schemes. Crypto assets cannot be sold short on the platform. Rules for crypto assets vary by jurisdiction and are changing, for example under the Markets in Crypto Assets Regulation in the European Union.

Copy trading risk

Copy trading mirrors the trades of a strategy profile in proportion to your allocation. Copied trades can lose money, and a copy can lose its whole allocation. Strategy profiles are shown without return figures, and no result is guaranteed. A copied account can behave differently from the strategy's history, and fills can differ from the prices the strategy achieved. A copy stop loss stops copying once the drawdown you set is reached, but it does not guarantee an exit at that level, because prices can gap. Copy trading is an optional feature and is not investment advice.

Liquidity, execution and price risk

Some instruments trade thinly, and a large order can move the price against you. Orders can fill at prices different from the price you saw, especially in fast markets, around news events or outside regular trading hours. A market order does not guarantee a price. A stop order becomes a market order when triggered and may fill well away from the stop price. Trading in extended hours carries wider spreads and lower liquidity. Some orders may not fill at all, or may expire under their time in force.

Market data and technology risk

Quotes may be delayed, and the platform labels delayed quotes. Market data can contain errors or be interrupted. Internet connections, devices, power supplies and third party services can fail, which may stop you from placing, changing or canceling orders. Cyber incidents, including phishing and account takeover, can cause losses. Keep your login details private and use the security features in your account settings.

Funding, withdrawal and settlement risk

Deposits and withdrawals are subject to review and approval, and funds can take time to appear in your account. Bank and cryptocurrency transfers can be delayed or lost if the details are wrong, and cryptocurrency transfers usually cannot be reversed. Stocks, ETFs and options settle one business day after the trade (T+1), and cryptocurrency trades settle immediately. Sale proceeds may not be available for withdrawal until settlement is complete. Funds held for open orders, copy allocations or pending withdrawals cannot be used for other purposes.

Protection and compensation

Cash and securities in your account may not be covered by investor compensation or deposit protection schemes, such as those in the United States, the United Kingdom or the European Union. Where cover exists, it may be limited in amount and scope. Losses caused by market movements or your own decisions are not reimbursed. Check which protection applies to your account before you deposit funds.

Tax and reporting

Gains and losses may be taxable in your country of residence. Statements and realized gains reports are provided to help you keep records and are not tax advice. Wash sale rules, short term and long term capital gains rules and reporting obligations apply in many jurisdictions. Consult a qualified tax adviser before you file.

Regulatory references

This disclosure refers to the following laws, rules and frameworks. Their requirements may apply differently to your account, and the platform terms explain how each one applies to you.

  • United States: Securities Exchange Act of 1934, Regulation T, Regulation SHO, SEC Rule 201, FINRA Rule 4210 on margin, FINRA Rule 2360 on options, and the Bank Secrecy Act on anti money laundering.
  • European Union: the Markets in Financial Instruments Directive II (MiFID II), the Markets in Crypto Assets Regulation (MiCA) and the Anti Money Laundering Directives.
  • United Kingdom: the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the rules of the Financial Conduct Authority.
  • International: the Financial Action Task Force Recommendations on anti money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism.

Your responsibilities

You decide whether trading is suitable for you. Consider your financial situation, experience, investment objectives and tolerance for loss before you trade. Only trade with money you can afford to lose. Review your orders, margin status and copy settings regularly. If you are unsure, seek independent financial advice.
